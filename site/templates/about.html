About Fortleben

How keikaku translates

Before it drafts a line, keikaku writes an analysis of the book: its people, its tone, what the author is doing. That analysis steers every stage after it. One model drafts. Two others read the draft separately, one against the original and one as English. An editing pass weighs their notes and decides what changes. A final read goes over the whole text.

keikaku works like a translation house. Its method comes from literary translation practice and how publishing houses work, not only from machine-translation benchmarks.

How to read an edition

Press Aa to choose how you read.

  • Clean is like a printed novel: the text and the few notes you need.
  • Curious marks every note, with the Japanese under each paragraph when you want it.
  • Annotated sits side by side with the Japanese, with notes open under each paragraph and paragraph numbers in the margin.

Under More settings you can turn furigana and paragraph numbers on or off, and choose whether notes open under each paragraph. Open any paragraph marked * to see what keikaku's readers queried, what changed and why.

Leave a note in the margin

Anyone can scribble in the margin of any paragraph; a name is optional. Each note is checked before others see it, and then it sits beside its paragraph, like a well-worn library book.

Become an editor

Sign up, pick a book, plug in your own Meta key and steer the translation. Read keikaku's analysis of the book before it drafts, approve it, and keikaku translates the whole book. Your edition is published under your name, or as “an editor” if you would rather not be named, in a colour you pick.

Suggest a book

Paste a link to any work on Aozora Bunko on the wishlist. Editors add books from there as they are ready.

Where the texts come from

The Japanese comes from Aozora Bunko's free library of public-domain texts. Other languages will need their own source library and reading aids, the way Japanese needs furigana. Editions are free to share and adapt under CC BY-SA 4.0.
