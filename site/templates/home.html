Thousands of Japanese books sit free on Aozora Bunko and have never been translated into English. Most never will be. Fortleben publishes them in English, translated by a house of AI agents, with every query and decision behind each line a tap away.

Who translates?
keikaku, a translation house of AI agents on Meta's models. One drafts. Two read the draft separately, one against the Japanese and one as English. A desk editor weighs their notes and decides what changes, and a final read goes over the whole text.
Can I trust it?
It's machine work, and the page says so. The readers' notes and the desk's reasons sit beside every paragraph, so you can see what was argued over and judge for yourself. Where an editor has signed a paragraph off, it carries their mark.
Which languages?
Japanese into English. The house is built one source language at a time, each with its own library and reading aids; Japanese is the first.
Why the name?
Fortleben is Walter Benjamin's word for the life a work goes on to have in translation. Nothing here is a final text: editions change, and every version is kept.
