Most of the world's books have never been translated, and most never will be. Fortleben publishes public-domain literature as living translations.

keikaku, an AI translation engine, writes the first draft. Readers can steer it, leave notes in the margin and ask for another reading.

Fortleben is German for “living on”. Walter Benjamin used it for the life a work goes on to have in translation.

Who translates?keikaku, running on Meta's models. It is organised like a translation house: a translator drafts, readers comment, an editor decides. The method comes from literary translation practice and how publishing houses work. More on the About page.
How do I read it?Choose your reading view, from a clean text like a printed novel to the Japanese alongside. Open any paragraph to see the reasoning behind it, and the notes other readers have left in the margin.
Can I produce my own edition?Yes. Sign up, pick a book, plug in your own Meta key and steer the translation. The edition is published under your name.
