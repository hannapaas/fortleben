Most of the world's books have never been translated, and most never will be. Fortleben translates public-domain literature so anyone can read any text, and shows how each translation was made.

Who translates?
keikaku, a set of AI agents that drafts, checks against the original and revises before anything is published. Where a person has checked a paragraph, it says so. Where a person has translated the same work, we say so and name them.
Which languages?
Japanese into English, from Aozora Bunko's free library of public-domain texts. keikaku is built to work between other languages too; each one needs its own source library and its own reading aids, the way Japanese needs furigana.
Why the name?
Fortleben is Walter Benjamin's word for the life a work goes on to have in translation. Nothing here is final: every edition keeps changing, and every version is kept.
