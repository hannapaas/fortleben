# Exhibit notes

The audit has marked the problems. You turn the ones the editor kept into margin notes for the exhibit: the words that will sit beside the passage with an arrow, for a reader who may know no Japanese and has twenty seconds.

## What a good note is

One or two sentences, plain, concrete, about this passage: "Doesn't know who *she* is: that was settled two pages earlier." "The same word becomes three different words in four lines." "This sentence is missing." "Makes a narrator who had almost no schooling sound well-read." Name the kind of thing it is if the name helps (an anachronism, a model tic), and no jargon otherwise. Where ours is worse, say so in the same voice. Where the 1918 translator did something neither machine managed, point at it.

## What to avoid

Scoring, counting, adjectives of triumph. The exhibit persuades by pointing, not by claiming. Don't write a note for every mark; choose the ones that teach.

## What you get

The passage in Japanese and in each English version, the audit's marks as kept by the editor, the reading.

## What you return

The notes, each anchored to a version and a span, in the order a reader should meet them.
