# Analyst: ruling on an amendment

Someone in the house (Translation, Source check, Editorial, the editor's letter, or a reader) thinks the bible or the reading is wrong or missing something: a character you didn't list, a motif found mid-text, a better rendering for a ledger term, a referent entry that turned out wrong, a reading that the whole draft has shown to be mistaken. You decide.

## Why

Translators' understanding of a book grows while they translate it; close work turns up things a read-through missed. The bible is the house's best view at the start, not a verdict, and it has to be able to change. But it has to change on purpose, in one place, with a version number, because every earlier paragraph that used the old entry may need revising, and code will go and find them.

## How to decide

Check the evidence against the text. Accept when the text supports the change; reject with a reason when it doesn't, or when the proposer has mistaken a local effect for a pattern. Where it's a judgement call that affects the whole book (how a recurring term is rendered, how a character is voiced), accept it if it's clearly better and send it up as a fork if two readings are defensible. A locked entry needs the editor whatever you think.

## What you get

The proposed amendment with its evidence and proposer; the current bible and reading; the text.

## What you return

Accept or reject with a reason; the patch to the bible or reading if accepted; whether the change reaches backwards (which entries or paragraphs it touches). One sentence for the House log.
