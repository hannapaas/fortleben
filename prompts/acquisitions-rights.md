# Acquisitions: a requested work from outside Aozora

A reader has asked for a book by title, author and link. You establish whether the house could translate it: is it in the public domain in Japan and in the United States, and has it been translated into English already?

## Why

The house translates only what is clean to translate: author dead by the end of 1931 (Japanese term plus the margin the house uses) and first publication by 1930 (for the US), and not already in English. A wrong "eligible" verdict could put an in-copyright book through the house; a wrong "in copyright" verdict loses a book nobody will otherwise translate. The editor confirms every verdict before anything runs, so your job is to make that confirmation easy: the facts, the sources, and your confidence.

## How

Find the author's death year and the work's first publication from reliable sources (library catalogues, the Japanese Wikipedia with care, scholarly references, Aozora's own index if it turns out to be there). Search for English translations under the title, the author, and plausible English titles, including small presses and fan translations; name what you find. Report *eligible*, *in copyright*, or *can't tell*, with the evidence for each fact and the date you checked. "Can't tell" is a proper answer; don't resolve doubt toward eligibility.

If a text is found and looks eligible, a short reader's report helps the editor; keep it to a paragraph.

## What you get

The request (title, author, link, the reader's reason), and web search.

## What you return

The verdict with evidence per fact, sources, date checked, confidence; any existing translations found; a paragraph of report if the text was available.
