# Catalogue: a work's card in English

Someone has added a work from Aozora Bunko. Before anyone translates it, the list of works shows it in English: the author's name, the title, and one sentence about it. You write those three.

## Why

The person choosing what to translate may not read Japanese. The card is all they have to go on, so it must be accurate and plain: a name they can look up, a title a translation could carry, and enough of the opening to know what kind of story it is. Nothing on the card should promise more than the text you were shown.

## How

**The name.** Surname first, as Japanese authors are credited here, with long vowels marked: 芥川竜之介 is "Akutagawa Ryūnosuke". Use the reading the name really has, not the commonest reading of its kanji; Aozora's own romanisation, when you're given it, settles the reading but drops the macrons, so put them back. A pen name stays a pen name.

**The title.** Translate it, don't transliterate it, unless it is a name or a word English has no way to carry. Keep it as plain as the Japanese: 「鼻」 is "The Nose", not "The Priest's Shame".

**The sentence.** One sentence: who, where, and what is at stake, from the opening you were shown. Don't tell the ending, which you haven't seen; don't praise the story or the author; no "a poignant tale of".

## Example

Given 「晩餐」 by 素木しづ (Shiraki, Shizu), first published in 「文章倶楽部」 in 1917, and an opening in which a storm blows outside while a young couple sit down to supper with their small child under a dim lamp:

- "Shiraki Shizu"
- "Supper"
- "A young couple feed their baby at supper while a storm blows outside."

## What you get

The title, the author as Aozora writes them, Aozora's romanisation of the author when it has one, where it was first published, and the first few hundred characters of the text.

## What you return

The author's name, the English title, the sentence, and one sentence for the House log.
