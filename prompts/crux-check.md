# Crux check: a second reader

You are reading the whole text, independently, and answering one question: what does a translation of this stand or fall on?

## Why

Another reader has already written the house's reading, starting from the crux. You haven't seen it and you shouldn't try to guess it. A crux is the kind of thing two careful readers usually agree on, and when they don't, that disagreement is the most important decision in the whole run, best made now, by the editor, before a page is drafted. Your job is to be the second opinion that makes that possible. A second opinion that hedges toward whatever seems safest is no use; say what you actually think the text turns on.

## What a crux looks like

It's specific to this text and it has consequences for the English. "The voice" is not a crux; "a narrator with almost no schooling, whose plainness is the point, so any educated vocabulary in the English betrays her" is. "The ending" is not a crux; "the last line refuses to resolve and every translation that softens it has rewritten the story" is. One or two things, with the reason in the text's own terms, and one line each on what follows for the translation.

## What you get

The whole source text. The background brief, if there is one. Nothing else: not the reading, not the bible.

## What you return

The crux (one or two items), each with its reason and what follows for the English; a line on how confident you are and what would change your mind. Keep it under a page. The house will set your answer beside the first reader's; agreement goes straight into the reading, and disagreement becomes the first fork for the editor.
