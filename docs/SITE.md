# Fortleben — the site, page by page

Written 1 Nov 2026, brought in line with the code 3 Nov. What a visitor finds at each address and what they can do there, as `house/web.py` serves it. Anything not in the table isn't served: it 404s. This is the contract for the site lane; `CONCEPT.md` says why, `STAGES.md` says how the text got there.

Two rules run through every page:
- **Nothing hinges on a human.** Every text is useful as machine work. Nothing unfinished is published: every edition on the site has been through keikaku's readers and editing pass, and says so once, at the top. A paragraph carries a mark only when an editor has signed it off: *editor-approved*, in words. The site says so plainly rather than implying more.
- **No dollars on the site.** Effort shows in drops of ink (`RUNNING-A-TEXT.md` §1), and a run on someone's own key in tokens. API prices and keikaku's recipes will keep changing, and a dollar figure on a page goes stale; the dollar figures live in the README and the evals, for developers and judges.

## Comments, reactions, proposals

Three different things, kept apart because they measure different things. Only comments are built.

- **Comments** are marginalia: per paragraph, written in the right-hand margin beside it on wide screens and under it on phones, flat (no replies), with an optional name and no account. They're kept apart from the translation's own notes and never change the text.
- **Reactions** (not built) would be quick and anonymous: *beautiful · confusing · sounds off · made me stop* for how it reads, and *I think this is wrong* (which asks for a reason) for whether it's right. Never as rankings, and a reaction would change nothing by itself.
- **Proposals** (not built) would: select a span, suggest a replacement, pick a reason. Once screened, a note to the desk that comes back *taken*, *declined* or *forked* with a reason. Answering costs a model call, so they'd be capped per day.

**What moderation means here.** No human gate. Free checks in code come first: a low rate limit per cookie and per IP (the connection's own address, or behind Railway's proxy the one it appended to `X-Forwarded-For`, read only with `KEIKAKU_TRUST_PROXY=true`; `ARCHITECTURE.md` §8), a daily cap across the site, a length limit and a cap on links. A comment that passes them is screened in the background by Spark (the `reader-proposal` prompt), the name included, for spam, abuse, personal data and anything unfit to publish, and for being plainly unrelated to the book. Until it passes, only its writer sees it; if it fails, they see why and can reword. Every published comment has a *report* link (reports have their own rate limit per address, a little higher than comments'); reports from two different addresses hide it at once. Nothing is deleted: a failed or hidden comment stays in the database with its reason, and simply disappears from the page. Once a day the editor gets one email (through Resend) listing the day's comments, what the screen stopped and what readers hid, each with a signed link to hide or publish it; the link opens a page that asks, and only its button acts. `keikaku comments` lists, hides and restores from the command line. Where `KEIKAKU_CONTACT` is set, the site shows it as an address to write to (on the reset page, when reset mail isn't set up). This keeps to the rule that nothing hinges on a person being available, and it means the site behaves the same during judging week whether or not anyone is watching it.

## How it grows

Not a page on the site; the thinking behind it, honest enough to become one:

- **Requests and votes decide what's next; an editor decides what runs.** No self-serve translation for now: the rights check, the cost and the moderation all need a person in the loop, and there's one. A text with no editor still runs, and its edition says the reading and voice weren't reviewed.
- **The wanted list is a public signal.** Which untranslated books readers want, including in-copyright ones we'll never touch, is useful to translators and presses. We'd rather a person translated them.
- **Funding a book.** The plan, not yet built: a book on the wanted list can carry a target; once readers have contributed that much, it's run through the house and published for everyone, free, as every edition here is. Nobody buys a translation; they fund one for everyone.
- **Supporting the house.** Also a plan: EPUB editions sold at a modest price on the usual stores, as a way to give something back. Everything in them is free here and anyone can generate their own, which the listing will say.
- **Other languages.** Japanese is first because it's the editor's own reading. The house is built per source language: a text comes in through a source adapter (Aozora is one), the story bible and the departments are the same, and what's specific to a language (furigana and ruby for Japanese, say) is added as that language arrives.
- **What stays the same as it grows:** every line labelled, nothing ranked by popularity, no human-sounding personas, public-domain sources only, never "human-quality".
