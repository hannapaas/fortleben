# 7. Readers who propose rewrites

**Question.** The stiffness sits in sentences the readers flag and the desk only patches. If the readers may propose the rewritten sentence or passage, does the desk end up with better English?

**Changes, all to prompts:**
- **Both readers may suggest "the whole sentence or passage if that's what it takes"** instead of "a wording only if one is obvious". The readable reader is told to rewrite when "the shape of the English is the problem".
- **The desk prompt loses "Change the span, not the paragraph".** It keeps "Don't rewrite paragraphs to your own taste".
- **The drafting prompt now says notes are for lines that look wrong or stiff and are faithful,** and that most pages need only a few. The drafter's notes went from 2 paragraphs to 13.

**Set-up.** One fresh draft, then two versions on it: Spark readers (A) and Wonder readers (B), each with a Wonder desk. They're audited blind with the one-shot and with experiment 6's best version (Wonder readers and the old prompts, on the previous draft).

## What happened

- **No improvement.** A and B have as many serious stiff sentences as before and more minor ones. The auditor called them "a light reworking of the same draft". The knotted sentences in p12, p13 and p16 survived.
- **The comparison is muddied.** A and B started from a new draft, made with the edited drafting prompt, and experiment 6's best didn't. So this doesn't separate the readers' rewrites from the new draft.
- **Spark and Wonder readers came out level this time.** In experiment 6 Wonder came first; here Spark is a place ahead. That points to noise, not a real difference between the models.
- **Every house version kept the meaning,** with no serious fidelity slips. The one-shot came last, because it gives the bowl to the child and flattens "her heart bowed down".

## What changed

Nothing yet. The clean test is the new reader prompts on the previous draft, which separates the two changes.
