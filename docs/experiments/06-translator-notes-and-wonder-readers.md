# 6. The translator's notes at the desk, and Wonder readers

**Questions.**
1. **Does the desk decide better if it sees the translator's notes?** A line that is odd on purpose (her heart "bowed down") draws a note against it from the readable reader and none for it. The desk then sees only one side.
2. **Do Wonder readers do better than Spark readers?**

**Set-up.** The same draft for every run: run 18's plain first draft, served from the cache, so only the readers and the desk vary.

| Version | Readers | Desk sees the translator's notes |
|---|---|---|
| Run 18 | Spark | no |
| A | Spark | yes |
| B | Wonder | yes |

## What happened

- **A changed almost nothing.** The plain drafter left notes on only 2 of 33 paragraphs, so the desk had little to read. The 8 paragraphs that differ from run 18 are the desk's own variation from run to run.
- **B's Wonder readers left fewer, longer notes:** 12, against Spark's 24. They found things Spark's readers didn't. For example, 安らかさ is varied as "peace", "rest" and "restfulness" where the author repeats one word, and "Young as she was" adds a concession the Japanese doesn't make. Neither set of readers caught the rhetorical なんだらう ("what do they matter"). The readers cost $0.22, against $0.10 for Spark's.
- **B's first desk answer was empty.** It was valid against the schema but decided none of the 12 notes. The house now asks again when a desk answer decides none of its notes. The second answer was a full one: 11 notes taken and 1 declined.

**A blind audit of all four, with the one-shot:**

| | Fidelity (critical / serious / minor) | Translationese (serious / minor) | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B**, Wonder readers | 0 / 0 / 4 | 4 / 16 | 1 |
| One-shot | 0 / 2 / 6 | 2 / 14 | 2 |
| Run 18, Spark readers | 0 / 1 / 4 | 4 / 17 | 3 |
| A, Spark readers, desk sees notes | 0 / 1 / 5 | 5 / 18 | 4 |

- **B was the most faithful version so far, and the only one that kept every one of her images:** "her heart had already bowed down, lonely", "the bottom of her eyes". With fewer notes against the draft, fewer of its right but odd choices were edited away.
- **The three house versions share one draft, and they're stiff in the same places** (p8, p9, p12, p13). The readers flag those sentences, and the desk patches them without rebuilding them.
- **The auditor's summary:** "The best publishable text would be S's fidelity with U's sentence-level fixes" (S is B; U is the one-shot).

## What changed

- **The desk sees the translator's notes.** It costs nothing, and it will matter once the drafter writes them where a line looks wrong but is faithful.
- **A desk answer that decides none of its notes is asked for again, once.**
- **Each response is recorded piece by piece** (message parts and their length), so an odd answer can be diagnosed afterwards.
- **Wonder readers are worth a second look.** That's one audit and one draft, so it isn't settled.
